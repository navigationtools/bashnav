**Bearings are relative to the other vessel.** 0 means you are looking at her
bow, 090 at her starboard side, 180 at her stern.

## The lamp letters

**W** white  **R** red  **G** green  **Y** yellow — beside every lamp, so the
pattern reads with no colour at all. **fl** flashes, at 120 a minute or more,
and the rate is part of the identification.

## Lights worth knowing cold

| | |
|---|---|
| Masthead, sidelights, sternlight | power-driven, under way |
| Two mastheads, after one higher | power-driven, 50 m or more |
| Sidelights and sternlight alone | sailing |
| Red over red | not under command |
| Red, white, red | restricted in her ability to manoeuvre |
| Red, red, red | constrained by her draught |
| Green over white | trawling |
| Red over white | fishing, other than trawling |
| White over red | pilot vessel on duty |
| All-round white, forward | at anchor, under 50 m |

## Sidelight cut-off

**±2.5°, not exact.** Annex I allows up to 3° outside the prescribed sector, so
there is a small arc right ahead where **both** sidelights show. Do not call
dead-ahead off one sidelight alone.

## Bearing drift — the whole of it

> **Away from your bow:** she passes astern, or never crosses your track.
> **Toward your bow:** she crosses ahead, or draws ahead and clears.

No exceptions in 700,000 geometries. Relative motion is a straight line, so
the bearing sweeps one way and cannot come back.

**Good drift and still not safe — Rule 7(d)(ii):**

1. a very large vessel — you plotted her bridge, her bow is nearer;
2. a tow — the tug clears, the barge on the wire does not;
3. close range — drift goes as the square of the range.

## The report

**Designation, bearing, drift, range, CPA, time.** Bearing *before* drift.
Say **"steady"** out loud — silence could mean you have not looked.

## The arithmetic

| | |
|---|---|
| Three-minute rule | yards travelled in 3 min = speed in knots × 100 |
| Six-minute rule | miles in 6 min = speed in knots ÷ 10 |
| CPA estimate | 1° at 1 mile ≈ 35 yards |
| Ekelund | R(kyd) = 1.91 × ΔS⊥(kt) / Δω(°/min) |
| | S⊥ = own speed × sin(own course − bearing) |

## Spoken bearing styles

| | |
|---|---|
| `rn` | "bearing 311, Red 20, drawing left" |
| `usn` | "bearing 311, Port 20, drawing left" |
| `rel360` | "bearing 311, 340 relative" |
| `words` | "bearing 311, fine on the port bow" |
| `none` | "bearing 311" |

The true bearing is always given: it is what goes on the chart and what
agrees with the radar.

## What it will not do

1. It does not replace the Convention. Where they differ, the Convention is
   right.
2. It has no radar, no AIS and no position. Every exercise is generated.
3. It will not tell you that a situation is safe. It tells you what the rules
   require and what the geometry implies; Rule 2 is still yours.
