## The display: day, dark and night

The browser build has three display modes. They are not decoration: one of
them exists to protect your night vision, and choosing the wrong one at the
wrong moment costs you something real.

| Mode | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **NIGHT** | Red on black, for a dark-adapted eye. The default. |
| **DARK** | Near-black with light text, for below decks, dusk, or indoors. |
| **DAY** | White with near-black text, for daylight and for printing. |

### Why night is the default

A tool you open on deck in the dark must not start bright. A bright panel
costs a dark-adapted eye roughly **twenty minutes** to recover from, and
twenty minutes is a long time to be unable to see a mark or a ship's lights
properly. So the first time you open any of these tools you get night, and
from then on you get whatever you last chose.

### Three buttons, not a cycle

The three modes are three separate buttons, and the one you are in is shown
as selected. That is deliberate.

1. **A cycle can take you somewhere you did not intend.** One stray press
   with a cycling control can land you on DAY at three in the morning.
2. **A cycle cannot tell you where you are**, only what the next press would
   do. Three buttons show the current mode at a glance without changing it.
3. **Going darker is always one tap.** DARK and NIGHT are immediate from
   anywhere, because going darker never costs you anything.
4. **Going from NIGHT to DAY takes a deliberate half-second press.** Hold the
   DAY button and it fills as it goes; let go early and nothing happens.
   Nothing bright appears on the screen until the change actually completes.
   That one transition is guarded because that one transition is the only one
   that costs you your dark adaptation.

### The setting follows you

The mode is remembered for all of the tools together, and for the course
pages that embed them. Set night in one and you are in night in the next.
It is remembered in the browser, so it survives closing the app.

### What the display can and cannot do in sunlight

Worth knowing before you blame the app on a bright day.

In sunlight, light reflecting off the glass lands on the dark parts of the
screen and the light parts alike. That lifts everything together and flattens
the difference between them, so the contrast you actually see collapses no
matter what colours the page uses:

| Condition | Body text, as seen |
|---|---|
| Indoors | about 10:1 |
| Overcast on deck | about 6:1 |
| Bright | about 3.5:1 |
| Full sun on the glass | about 2.3:1 |

**At the extreme, every colour scheme converges.** Pure black on pure white
measures the same 2.3:1 in full sun as the day mode does, because the glare
swamps the difference. A different palette is not the answer, and nor is a
different shade of white: across every plausible choice of background the
contrast changes by under 4%, which is below what an eye can see.

**What does work, in order:**

1. **Turn the screen brightness up.** This is the one large lever, and it is
   the device's, not the app's.
2. **Shade the screen** with a hand, your body or the sprayhood. Removing the
   reflection is worth more than any change the app can make.
3. **Use DAY mode**, which at least puts the maximum luminance the panel can
   give behind the darkest text it can draw.

### A note for printing

Documents and their figures are made in **day** mode. A screen captured in
dark or night mode prints as a solid block of toner and loses the fine type
in a working, so the printed pages show the day display even if that is not
the one you use at sea.
